This tab is your punch list

A new domain is too big to memorize. Read every Critical and Warning card, then skim Info. Each card tells you why it matters and what to do next — not just that something is true.

Warning Availability

1 DC(s) could not be queried remotely

NW-RODC01: WinRM cannot complete the operation. Verify the computer is up and WinRM is configured.

Why it matters. WinRM, firewall, or credential problems hide roles, disks, and service health.
What to do next. Confirm WinRM (5985/5986) and that your account is local admin on those DCs. AD data in this report is still valid.
Info FSMO

All FSMO roles sit on one DC

Holder: NW-DC01.northwind.lab

Why it matters. Convenient, and common, but that one DC is now a special snowflake. Losing it means seizing roles.
What to do next. Keep roles on a pair of well-known hub DCs. Document who owns them and how you would seize.
Warning Forest

AD Recycle Bin is not enabled

Deleted users, groups, and computers are not recoverable with full attributes after tombstone processing.

Why it matters. This is the first optional feature most new owners enable. It cannot be turned off once enabled.
What to do next. Enable-ADOptionalFeature "Recycle Bin Feature" after you confirm forest functional level is 2008 R2+.
Critical Replication

NW-DC01 -> NW-RODC01: RPC server unavailable (1722)

Why it matters. If DCs disagree, passwords, group membership, and GPOs become location-dependent. That is how "it works in this building" starts.
What to do next. Start with repadmin /replsummary and the failing partner pair. Fix DNS and connectivity before you seize roles.

Why it matters. Empty sites are valid (client-only sites) if subnets still exist so clients locate a nearby DC.

Warning Identity

Default minimum password length is 8

Modern baselines start at 12–15, plus ban-list / Windows Hello / gMSA for services.

Why it matters. Length beats complexity theater. Fine-grained policies can raise length for admins first.
What to do next. Review Default Domain Policy and any FGPP before you tighten anything — you can lock out service accounts.
Warning Identity

Account lockout threshold is 0 (never lock out)

Brute-force password guesses never lock the account.

Why it matters. A threshold of 0 is sometimes kept to avoid DoS lockouts. Know which it is.
What to do next. Prefer smart lockout (Azure) or a modest threshold plus a short observation window.
Warning Identity

krbtgt password is 640 days old

krbtgt is the account that encrypts Kerberos TGTs. A stolen hash remains useful until this password is rotated twice.

Why it matters. Microsoft guidance is a two-stage reset (then wait for replication / ticket lifetime, then reset again).
What to do next. Use Microsoft KRBTGT reset guidance. Never treat this as a casual Set-ADAccountPassword.
Warning Identity

Domain Admins has 6 members

Administrator, j.blake, s.patel, helpdesk.admin, vendor.temp, backup.svc

Why it matters. DA is the keys to the domain. Broad membership is the most common inherited-domain smell.
What to do next. Inventory each member, move day-to-day work to lesser groups, and keep DA for DC/domain work only.
Warning Identity

Enterprise Admins has 2 member(s)

Administrator, j.blake

Why it matters. These forest-wide groups should be empty in daily operations. Membership is a break-glass event.
What to do next. Remove standing membership. Use PAM / privileged access workstations / just-in-time groups.
Info Identity

DnsAdmins is populated

netops, dhcp.svc

Why it matters. DnsAdmins can be abused for privilege escalation on DCs that run DNS (load a DLL as dns.exe).
What to do next. Treat DnsAdmins as tier-0. Confirm every member still needs it.
Info Hygiene

Stale enabled accounts are extra passwords an attacker can try, plus license and audit noise.

Why it matters. Disable, not delete, until you confirm they are not service or seasonal accounts.
What to do next. Start with a disable + description stamp. Deletion comes after a sitting period.
Info Hygiene

Stale computer accounts still have passwords and SPNs. They clutter GPOs and inventories.

Why it matters. Disable first. Confirm they are not clustered VMs or rarely-booted appliances.
What to do next. Use LastLogonDate as a hint, not gospel — it is not replicated in real time.

Why it matters. Unconstrained delegation lets that host request tickets to any service as the inbound user. It is a tier-0 risk.
What to do next. Prefer constrained or resource-based constrained delegation. DCs have this by design — focus on member servers.

FSMO means "this write can only happen in one place"

Most AD writes replicate multimaster. Five operations do not. If you inherit a domain, memorize the holders and keep them on DCs you would actually answer a page for.

Forest FSMO

Schema Master

NW-DC01.northwind.lab

The only DC that can write to the AD schema (new classes and attributes).

Schema updates are rare (Exchange, AD Connect, Windows functional-level raises) but they must succeed.

Keep it on a healthy, well-connected hub DC. Treat schema extensions as change-controlled events.
Forest FSMO

Domain Naming Master

NW-DC01.northwind.lab

The only DC that can add or remove domains and application partitions.

Idle most of the time. Required when creating domains, trusts that add partitions, or some DNS app partitions.

Usually colocated with Schema Master. Confirm it is reachable from wherever you would run domain-create tools.
Domain FSMO

PDC Emulator

NW-DC01.northwind.lab

Password changes and lockouts, time source for the domain, GPO editing target, backward-compat PDC.

This is the FSMO you will feel immediately if it is down: logons, lockouts, and time drift.

Put it on your most reliable, lowest-latency DC. Clients and other DCs sync time from here (directly or indirectly).
Domain FSMO

RID Master

NW-DC01.northwind.lab

Hands each DC pools of Relative IDs used to build new SIDs for users, computers, and groups.

If it is offline long enough that RID pools exhaust, that DC cannot create new security principals.

Short outages are fine. Multi-day outages plus a hiring/build surge are not. Confirm every DC has a RID pool.
Domain FSMO

Infrastructure Master

NW-DC01.northwind.lab

Updates cross-domain group-to-user references (phantoms) so memberships stay consistent.

In a single-domain forest this role is effectively idle. In multi-domain forests it matters.

Do not place it on a GC unless every DC in the domain is a GC (the usual modern design).
